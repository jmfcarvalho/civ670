MODULE 01 — STRATEGIC FOUNDATIONS

The Innovation Plan: Architecture & Systemic Scope

Strategic Anatomy of the Innovation Plan

The Innovation Plan translates lab-scale technological validation into an operationalized market-entry document, articulating eight interdependent modules:

1. Justification: Core problem, persona pain & market impact.
2. Persona & Value: Targeted jobs-to-be-done & pain relievers.
3. Objectives & Targets: Quantifiable, time-bounded KPI thresholds.
4. Hypotheses: Critical assumptions & prototype proofs.
5. Team Structure: Multidisciplinary roles & execution skills.
6. Solution & Differentiator: TRL rating & competitive moats.
7. External Barriers: Regulatory codes, IP constraints & policies.
8. Strategic Partnerships: University-industry co-development.
Covers 12-month development horizon
MARKET ENGINE

Hybrid Innovation Model

Hard engineering ventures must synthesize Technology-Push (academic discovery) and Market-Pull (industrial demand) to escape the Valley of Death:

Technology-Push (R&D Rigor): Scientific novelty, patented processes, and technical feasibility derived from doctoral and master's research.
Market-Pull (Customer Discovery): Unaddressed compliance mandates, field downtime costs, and commercial willingness to pay.

MODULE 02 — SYSTEMIC EQUILIBRIUM

The Innovation Sweet Spot: 4-Pillar Model

Holistic Equilibrium Architecture

Achieving the innovation sweet spot requires balancing four orthogonal criteria. Neglecting any single pillar results in venture collapse:

Desirability (Human): Solves real user pain; achieves strong product-market fit and customer pull.
Viability (Business): Economically sustainable model; positive unit economics and scalable margins.
Feasibility (Technology): Operational capability to manufacture, deliver, and maintain at scale.
Integrity (Impact): Environmental and ethical soundness; circularity, ESG compliance, and social license.
Tension: Profitability vs. Environmental Degradation
AUDIT METHODOLOGY

Decoupled Pillar Audit

Hard engineering solutions often fail because teams allow high technical feasibility to mask fatal flaws in customer desirability or environmental integrity:

Independent Assessment: Score each pillar on an isolated scale to expose hidden venture liabilities.
Continuous Iteration (Lean Cycle): Ideate $\rightarrow$ Prototype $\rightarrow$ Test $\rightarrow$ Refine across all four pillars simultaneously.

REAL-WORLD PARADOX

The Bottled Water Case Study

Bottled water demonstrates how severe asymmetry across the four pillars threatens long-term venture viability:

Desirability: HIGH Convenient access to clean, potable drinking water anywhere.
Viability: STRONG High recurring margins and continuous repeat purchase behavior.
Feasibility: HIGH Established blow molding, automated bottling, and distribution fleets.
Integrity: LOW (FATAL) Single-use plastic pollution, microplastics, high carbon footprint per liter.
Key Takeaway: Despite commercial success, low Integrity triggers regulatory bans, carbon taxes, and consumer backlash, threatening long-term survival.

MODULE 03 — EXECUTION INFRASTRUCTURE

Goals, Metrics & Multidisciplinary Team Architecture

The Strategic Measurement Triad

Hard engineering ventures translate technical ambition into milestones through three mathematically coupled layers:

OBJECTIVES
Broad Strategic Ambition: Defines the overarching technological and commercial destination (e.g., validate low-carbon binder at industrial scale).
INDICATORS
Key Performance Tracking Tools (KPIs): Standardized variables measuring progress (e.g., 28-day compressive strength, embodied $\text{kg CO}_2\text{e/m}^3$).
TARGETS
Quantifiable Thresholds: Time-bounded numerical limits (e.g., $f_{ck} \ge 40\text{ MPa}$ and carbon reduction $\ge 30\%$ by month 6).
Formal condition: $\text{Target} = f(\text{Indicator}, t_{\text{deadline}})$
FOUNDATIONAL CAPITAL

Team Supremacy Over Ideas

Execution capability dictates venture survival. High-performing engineering spin-offs prioritize team composition over isolated technical inventions:

Role Allocation & Accountability: Clear division across scientific R&D, pilot fabrication, regulatory certification, and finance.
Multidisciplinary Synergy: Deep technical know-how paired with market acumen prevents academic lock-in and commercial failure.

CAPABILITY AUDIT

Multidisciplinary Skill Balance

Hard tech ventures routinely fail not from technological limitations, but from missing functional capabilities:

Failure Mode 1: Technical Monoculture Teams consisting solely of laboratory researchers struggle with B2B negotiations, financial runway modeling, and regulatory filings.
Failure Mode 2: Premature Delegations Founders abdicating direct customer discovery to external sales consultants lose essential feedback required for prototype iteration.
A team of 3 complementary profiles (Hacker/Maker, Hustler/Commercial, Operator/Regulatory) outperforms any single genius founder.

MODULE 04 — ECOSYSTEM DYNAMICS

External Constraints, Barriers & Strategic Partnerships

External Constraints & Entry Hurdles

Engineering spin-offs encounter structural market frictions classified under Michael Porter's entry barrier taxonomy:

Capital Requirements: High initial CapEx for pilot plants, characterization equipment, and scaling.
Regulatory Hurdles: Mandatory technical compliance (ABNT, ASTM) and environmental licensing.
Technology Control: Pre-existing competitor patents, proprietary know-how, and locked raw materials.
Institutional Policy: University innovation bylaws (NIT-UFV), royalty splits, and conflict of interest rules.
FTO analysis mandatory before pilot scaling
ACCELERATION LEVER

University-Industry Synergies

Strategic alliances bypass entry barriers through resource pooling and institutional credibility:

Shared Infrastructure & Lab Access: Leveraging university characterization facilities (e.g., SICon/DEC-UFV) to reduce venture CapEx.
Mutual Commitment Architecture: Defining partner contributions, IP co-ownership boundaries, and pilot off-take agreements.

COMPETITIVE FRAMEWORK

Porter's Entry Barriers in Hard Tech

Every engineering Innovation Plan must detail defensive moats and offensive strategies against established market incumbent barriers:

Supply-Side Economies of Scale: Incumbents produce building materials at massive unit discounts; spin-offs counter via high-performance green premiums.
Customer Switching Costs: Contractors resist untested cementitious blends due to liability risk; spin-offs provide certified insurance and third-party lab proofs.
Restrictive Government Policy: Mandatory national standards (e.g., ABNT NBR); spin-offs must participate directly in technical normalization committees.
INSTITUTIONAL DRILL-DOWN (BRANCH Y=1)

University-Industry Co-Development & IP Governance

Brazilian Legal Framework & NIT-UFV Protocols

Structuring academic spin-offs requires explicit alignment with Brazilian Innovation Law (Law 10,973/04 & Law 13,243/16):

Intellectual Property Titularity: Patents generated using university public facilities remain under joint or institutional ownership (UFV/NIT), requiring formal licensing contracts.
Royalty Sharing Distribution: Mandatory statutory division of commercial licensing revenues among the academic inventors, research department, and university central fund.
Shared Laboratory Usage Agreements: Formal instruments enabling pre-incubation spin-offs to utilize university testing apparatuses without generating legal conflicts of interest.
Marco Legal da CT&I Compliant
PARTNER QUALIFICATION

Three-Point Partnership Audit

The Innovation Plan must evaluate each industrial prospective partner across three mandatory criteria:

1. Role & Scope Definition: Precise allocation of duties (e.g., pilot fabrication site, chemical feedstock provider, regulatory tester).
2. Resource Contributions: Quantification of economic contributions (in-kind engineering hours, industrial raw materials, co-financing grants).
3. Mutual Commitments & Exclusivity: Clear boundaries regarding commercial exclusivity, geographic reach, and first right of refusal for licensing.
Unvetted corporate partners create IP encumbrance that repels future venture capital rounds.

MODULE 04 — ECOSYSTEM DYNAMICS

External Constraints, Barriers & Strategic Partnerships

Risk Assessment Matrix Architecture

Risk management in innovation planning identifies, classifies, and controls failure variables using orthogonal probability and consequence dimensions:

$$\text{Risk Criticality Index (RCI)} = \text{Probability (P)} \times \text{Impact (I)}$$
Probability Scale: Discrete likelihood categories: Low ($P=1$), Average/Medium ($P=2$), and High ($P=3$).
Impact Severity Scale: Systemic disruption levels: Insignificant ($I=1$), Moderate ($I=2$), and Severe ($I=3$).
ISO 31000 Compliant Risk Categorization
DECISION MECHANICS

Operational Mitigation Protocols

Hard engineering ventures must preemptively categorize project uncertainties into three auditable operational intervention levels:

Acceptable Tier ($\text{RCI} \le 2$): Routine operational monitoring; no budget allocation required.
Mitigation Tier ($3 \le \text{RCI} \le 4$): Active engineering control, redundant suppliers, and bench buffers.
Critical Tier ($\text{RCI} \ge 6$): Mandates fundamental pivot, architectural redesign, or formal legal revision.

RISK CONTROL PROTOCOL

Severity Matrix & Strategic Actions

Every engineering project risk must be scored and mapped to an auditable risk mitigation strategy:

High Probability $\times$ Severe Impact ($\text{RCI}=9$): Immediate showstopper. Requires re-engineering of the solution, non-infringement redesign, or restructuring raw material supply chains.
Average Probability $\times$ Moderate Impact ($\text{RCI}=4$): Establish dual-sourcing contracts, run parallel laboratory formulation trials, and build standard operating procedures (SOPs).
Low Probability $\times$ Insignificant Impact ($\text{RCI}=1$): Retained project risk with passive observation; logged into the quarterly risk registry.

MODULE 06 — OPERATIONAL DEPLOYMENT

Action Plan Engineering: The 5W2H Framework

The 7 Pillars of Operational Rigor

The 5W2H methodology transforms abstract innovation goals into auditable work packages, eliminating execution friction across 7 dimensions:

What (Scope): Specific technical deliverable or operational activity to be executed.
Why (Justification): Underlying strategic purpose, hypothesis test, or risk mitigation rationale.
Where (Location): Physical laboratory facility, pilot plant site, or cloud deployment venue.
When (Timeline): Exact calendar duration, start/end deadlines within the 12-month sprint.
Who (Ownership): Designated responsible team member accountable for completion.
How (Methodology): Experimental standard (ASTM/ABNT), fabrication procedure, or validation protocol.
How Much (Cost): Direct CapEx, OpEx, scholarships, and consumables feeding the cash-flow forecast.
Covers complete 12-month execution window
EXECUTION CONTROL

12-Month Staged Validation

Hard tech roadmaps link technical TRL escalation directly to quarterly capital disbursements, ensuring zero unfunded commitments:

TRL Stage-Gate Linking: Milestones define clear criteria: an activity must not trigger disbursement until preceding test proofs validate hypothesis viability.
Direct Budget Synthesis: Every single row of the 5W2H register automatically feeds the line-item grant financial reporting requirements.

AUDIT CRITERIA

5W2H Execution Quality Checklist

Grant evaluators (FINEP/FAPEMIG/EMBRAPII) reject vague innovation timelines. Ensure each entry satisfies these conditions:

  • Unambiguous Ownership: One single responsible person named per row; shared responsibility equals zero accountability.
  • Verifiable Completion: Deliverables must be binary (either the report/prototype exists or it does not).
  • Exhaustive Costing: All direct expenses (consumables, shipping, stipends) must sum directly to the total budget.

MODULE 07 — FINANCIAL ARCHITECTURE

Budget Planning, Capital Stacking & Runway

12-Month Financial Engineering

Budget planning translates the 5W2H operational schedule into a synchronized cash-flow forecast, mapping expenses against non-dilutive and dilutive capital sources:

$$\text{Cash Balance}(t) = \text{Cash}(t-1) + \sum \text{Inflows}(t) - \sum \text{5W2H Outflows}(t)$$
Inflow Synchronization: Public calls (FINEP, FAPEMIG), corporate counterpart investments, scholarships, and angel capital.
Expenditure Feed: Direct inputs from 5W2H work packages: personnel stipends, pilot materials, third-party lab testing, and IP filing fees.
Target Metric: Minimum 6 Months Sustainable Runway
VENTURE SOLVENCY

Multi-Source Capital Stacking

Relying exclusively on a single grant disbursement risks project paralysis during administrative reimbursement delays:

Public Economic Subvention: Covers CapEx instrumentation and dedicated researcher stipends without equity dilution.
Industrial Matching Funds: Validates market demand and finances scalable raw materials for pilot production runs.
Self-Funding / Pre-Incubation Seed: Bridges regulatory filing windows and emergency operational overhead.
