MODULE 01 — TECHNOLOGY READINESS & GOVERNANCE

TRL Escalation & Stage-Gate Process

ISO 16290 / NASA Stage-Gate Architecture

Sequential maturation pipeline systematically de-risking technological novelties prior to capital-intensive pilot scale deployment.

TRL 1–3 Research Phase

Scientific literature review, fundamental principles observed, and analytical proof of concept formulated.

Gate 1: Proof Verified
TRL 4–6 Development Phase

Component validation in laboratory rig followed by system alpha prototype testing in simulated operational conditions.

Gate 2: Pilot Validated
TRL 7–9 Deployment Phase

Full-scale prototype demonstration in field operating environment, regulatory conformity, and serial production.

Gate 3: Market Ready
Standard: ISO 16290:2013
MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION

Advancement Risk Index

Technological readiness progress is inversely penalized by technical debt and uncharacterized operational failure modes:

$$\text{TRL}_{\text{eff}} = \text{TRL} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{\sigma_{\text{uncert}}}{\sigma_{\text{crit}}}\right) \cdot \prod_{k=1}^{M} \left(1 - p_{\text{fail}, k}\right)$$

Stage Escalation Hurdle: Transitioning between gates requires uncertainty variance reduction ($\sigma_{\text{uncert}} \to 0$) through quantitative empirical stress testing.

AUDIT CRITERIA

TRL Stage-Gate Compliance Checklist

TRL 1 to 3 Transition Gate: Empirical evidence demonstrating that observed phenomena behave according to theoretical physical models. Mathematical predictions verified via basic analytical laboratory tests.
TRL 4 to 6 Transition Gate: Sub-scale breadboard components integrated into an alpha configuration. Physical prototype must withstand expected stress factors (temperature, load cycles, chemical exposure) in relevant environmental chambers.
TRL 7 to 9 Transition Gate: Full-scale prototype validated in a field pilot environment under real-world operational regimes. Formal third-party safety audits, ISO certifications, and failure modes elimination (FMEA RPN < 100).

GOVERNING RELATIONSHIP

Prototyping Trade-Off Index

Balancing dimensional resolution, lead-time velocity, and material mechanical integrity across manufacturing regimes:

$$\eta_{\text{proto}} = \frac{\sigma_{\text{ult}} \cdot \phi_{\text{res}}}{t_{\text{lead}} \cdot \left(\text{Cost}_{\text{tool}} + \dot{m} \cdot C_{\text{feed}}\right)}$$

Scaling Law: Additive regimes minimize upfront tooling costs ($\text{Cost}_{\text{tool}} \to 0$) but exhibit anisotropic inter-layer shear weaknesses compared to subtractive bulk milling.

MODULE 03 — ENVIRONMENTAL QUANTIFICATION ENGINE

Life Cycle Assessment (ISO 14040/44)

Standardized Life Cycle Inventory & Modular Stages

Comprehensive attributional mass and energy balance across product life stages under rigorous cut-off criteria (ISO 14044).

Upstream (A1–A3) Production Stage

Raw resource extraction (A1), logistics to factory gate (A2), and manufacturing conversion energy (A3).

Cradle-to-Gate
Downstream (A4–C4) Use & End-of-Life

Transport to site (A4), assembly (A5), service maintenance (B1–B5), demolition (C1), and landfill disposal (C4).

Cradle-to-Grave
Circular (Module D) Avoided Burdens

Net environmental credits exported beyond the system boundary via recycling, remanufacturing, and energy recovery.

Circular Offset
Standard: ISO 14040 / EN 15804+A2
IMPACT CHARACTERIZATION MODEL

Global Warming Potential ($\text{GWP}$)

Net equivalent greenhouse gas emissions integrated over a 100-year atmospheric residence horizon:

$$\text{GWP}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} m_i \cdot \text{EF}_i + \sum_{j=1}^{p} (m_j \cdot d_j \cdot \tau_j) - \eta_{\text{rec}} \cdot \text{GWP}_{\text{virgin}}$$

Decoupling Principle: Substitution of high clinker-factor binders with secondary supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs) aggressively lowers upstream Module A1 intensity.

ISO 14044 NORMATIVE COMPLIANCE

Allocation Procedures & Cut-Off Boundaries

1% Mass & Energy Cut-off Rule: All inputs and outputs where data are available must be incorporated. Flows accounting for less than 1% of total mass or non-renewable primary energy may be excluded, provided cumulative omitted streams do not exceed 5% of total category impact.
Multi-Output Allocation Hierarchy: Allocation must be avoided wherever feasible via sub-division of unit processes or system expansion. When unavoidable, multi-product emissions must be partitioned strictly following thermodynamic/physical properties or economic revenue shares.
Module D Substitution Integrity: Avoided burden credits can only be claimed when secondary materials achieve functional equivalence with virgin technical nutrients, minus the processing impacts required for end-of-waste state attainment.

ISO 14025 Validated

VERIFICATION CRITERIA

EPD Data Quality Index (DQI)

Calculated metric evaluating primary empirical data share and temporal/geographical boundary compliance:

$$\text{DQI}_{\text{audit}} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} w_k \left(1 - \frac{\delta_{\text{geo}} + \delta_{\text{time}} + \delta_{\text{tech}}}{15}\right) \ge \Lambda_{\text{threshold}}$$

Audit Gatekeeper: If primary industrial metering falls below 85% of total mass-energy allocations, third-party certification is denied until foreground data acquisition is completed.

TECHNICAL AUDIT PROTOCOLS

PCR Construction Materials Framework

Functional Unit Standardization (EN 15804): Must reflect engineering performance. Concrete mixtures require specifications per $1\,\text{m}^3$ linked to 28-day compressive strength classes ($f_{\text{ck}}$) and durability exposure regimes according to EN 206 / ABNT NBR 6118.
Mandatory Upstream Cut-off & Electricity Grid Mix: All background energy profiles must apply residual national energy grid models updated within the last 3 years. Co-generation credits require physical enthalpy partitioning rather than economic revenue splits.
Third-Party Verifier Independence (ISO 14025): Auditor cannot belong to the consulting firm that formulated the original LCA. Field site audits of industrial mixing plants and raw material certificates are legally binding conditions for public listing.

MODULE 05 — ENVIRONMENTAL CLAIMS & STANDARDIZATION

Eco-Labeling Taxonomy (ISO 14020, 14024, 14021, 14025)

ISO Multi-Tier Environmental Claim Architecture

Differentiating voluntary self-declarations from multi-attribute third-party seals and quantified life cycle disclosure metrics.

ISO 14024 Type I: Eco-Seals

Voluntary, multi-criteria, independent third-party verified pass/fail seal (e.g., EU Ecolabel, Blue Angel, Green Seal).

Third-Party Audit
ISO 14021 Type II: Self-Declared

Manufacturer claims without mandatory external verification (e.g., "Recyclable", "Low VOC"). Highly prone to greenwashing.

Self-Declaration
ISO 14025 Type III: EPD Profiling

Quantified, modular life-cycle environmental data conforming to PCR rules, audited by approved program operators.

Full LCA Metrics
ISO 14020 Umbrella System
GOVERNANCE METRIC

Claim Robustness Index (CRI)

Quantifying legal and technical defensibility against unsubstantiated environmental marketing:

$$\text{CRI} = \alpha_{\text{audit}} \cdot \left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot \delta_{\text{metric}, i}}{\text{Scope}_{\text{total}}}\right) \cdot (1 - \gamma_{\text{vague}})$$

Audit Gatekeeper: Claims lacking third-party audit ($\alpha_{\text{audit}} = 0.35$) and relying on vague lexical statements ($\gamma_{\text{vague}} > 0.40$) face immediate regulatory sanctions under ISO 14021.

NORMATIVE DEFINITIONS

ISO 14020 Family Verification Rules

Type I Eco-Labels (ISO 14024): Must be based on pre-set, publicly accessible criteria formulated by independent scientific panels. Criteria must cover multiple environmental life stages (extraction, toxics, emissions, circularity) and enforce periodic facility re-inspections.
Type II Self-Declared Claims (ISO 14021): Explicitly forbids ambiguous claims such as "environmentally friendly", "green", or "nature-safe". Specific numerical limits and verifiable test methodologies (e.g., ISO 14021 Clause 7 on post-consumer recycled content) must be publicly accessible on demand.
Type III Environmental Declarations (ISO 14025): Does not set a threshold of good or bad; provides objective, comprehensive life-cycle impact indicators without subjective scores, enabling direct business-to-business (B2B) benchmarking against PCR criteria.

QUANTITATIVE CIRCULARITY METRIC

Material Circularity Indicator (MCI)

Quantifying closed-loop mass retention, unrecoverable waste leakage, and operational utility:

$$\text{MCI} = 1 - L(V, W) \cdot F(X) = 1 - \left(\frac{V + W}{2M + \frac{W_F - W_C}{2}}\right) \cdot \frac{0.9}{X}$$

Design Principle: Eliminating virgin feedstock ($V \to 0$) while optimizing component disassembly ensures linear flow penalty $L \to 0$, driving $\text{MCI} \to 1.0$.

C2CPII VERIFICATION CRITERIA

Cradle to Cradle 5 Performance Categories

1. Material Health: Chemical inventory down to 100 ppm (0.01%). Elimination of all x-assessed priority toxic substances (carcinogens, mutagens, reproductive toxins, persistent halogens) to ensure nutrient non-toxicity.
2. Product Circularity: Active cycling pathway definition. Technical components must document standardized disassembly protocols, reverse logistics contracts, and functional upcycling pathways without property loss.
3. Clean Air & Climate Protection: 100% renewable electricity utilization during manufacturing stages (A3) and verified greenhouse gas emissions offsets for remaining direct process loads.
4. Water & Soil Stewardship: Effluent discharges leaving industrial facilities must be clean enough to meet drinking water or pristine natural aquatic standards, preventing eutrophication and toxic accumulation.
