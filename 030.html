MODULE 01 — FOUNDATIONS

Definition, Scope & Deliverables

Tangible Products

Physical outcomes of engineering design and manufacturing (e.g., infrastructure, machines, materials, smart devices).

  • Output-oriented deliverables
  • Physical lifecycle: production to disposal
  • Directly measurable and storable

Intangible Services

Activity-based offerings involving engineering expertise, support, monitoring, and specialized consulting.

  • Process-oriented interaction
  • Value delivered continuously over time
  • Focus on availability and uptime
CONVERGENCE PARADIGM

Product-Service Systems (PSS)

Integrated solutions combining physical hardware with real-time value-added services.

INTERACTIVE TAXONOMY (BRANCH Y=1)

Product-Service Systems (PSS) Breakdown

01. Product-Oriented

Ownership with Support

Traditional sale of physical product with maintenance contracts, spare parts, and warranty agreements.

e.g., Heavy Machinery + Maintenance SLA Tap card for details
02. Use-Oriented

Access over Ownership

Product ownership remains with provider; user purchases availability via leasing, renting, or sharing.

e.g., Structural Sensor Network Leasing Tap card for details
03. Result-Oriented

Performance Contracts

Client pays strictly for quantified outcomes or functional utility without asset ownership.

e.g., Pay-per-m³ Concrete Pumped Tap card for details
Reference: Tukker, A. (2004). Eight types of product-service system: Eight ways to sustainability? Business Strategy and the Environment.
PSS ARCHETYPE 01

Product-Oriented System

The customer buys the physical system and retains title of ownership. Economic value is expanded post-sale through structured services.

Key Components: Preventive maintenance contracts, condition monitoring, guaranteed supply of OEM spare parts, and operator training.
PSS ARCHETYPE 02

Use-Oriented System

The physical asset remains on the provider's balance sheet. The user purchases access and operating availability over fixed periods.

Operating Model: Equipment leasing, shared machinery pools, and sensor fleet rental where maintenance and insurance are internalized by the provider.
PSS ARCHETYPE 03

Result-Oriented System

No product or machine is sold. The client enters a contract strictly for a quantified performance outcome or delivered functional utility.

Contract Examples: Guaranteed indoor temperature/air quality levels, pay-per-cubic-meter of treated water, or structural integrity assurance agreements.
MODULE 02A — ECONOMIC METHODOLOGY

Life Cycle Costing (LCC)

Discounted Cash Flow Formulation

$$\text{LCC} = C_{\text{cap}} + \sum_{t=1}^{N} \frac{C_{\text{op}}(t) + C_{\text{maint}}(t)}{(1+r)^t} + \frac{C_{\text{disp}}}{(1+r)^N}$$
$C_{\text{cap}}$ (CAPEX): Initial capital cost of design, engineering, materials, acquisition, and commissioning ($t=0$).
$C_{\text{op}}(t)$ (OPEX): Annual operational expenses including energy, staffing, and consumables in year $t$.
$C_{\text{maint}}(t)$ (Maintenance): Preventive maintenance, condition monitoring, component replacements, and unscheduled repairs in year $t$.
$C_{\text{disp}}$ (Disposal/End-of-Life): Decommissioning, demolition, recycling logistics, minus net salvage value.
$r$ & $N$: Real discount rate ($r$) and overall analytical service life horizon in years ($N$).

MODULE 02B — ENVIRONMENTAL METHODOLOGY

Life Cycle Assessment (ISO 14040/44 & EN 15978)

Global Warming Potential (GWP) Formulation

$$\text{GWP}_{\text{total}} = \text{GWP}_{\text{A1-A3}} + \text{GWP}_{\text{A4-A5}} + \text{GWP}_{\text{B1-B7}} + \text{GWP}_{\text{C1-C4}} - \text{GWP}_{\text{Module D}}$$
A1–A3 Product Stage: Raw material extraction (A1), upstream transport (A2), and product manufacturing (A3).
A4–A5 Construction Stage: Freight transport to site (A4) and on-site assembly/erection energy (A5).
B1–B7 Use Stage: Maintenance, repair, replacements (B1–B5) and operational energy/water consumption (B6–B7).
C1–C4 End-of-Life Stage: Deconstruction (C1), transport (C2), waste processing (C3), and final disposal (C4).
Module D (Beyond Boundary): Circular reuse benefits, secondary materials recovery, and avoided loads.

MODULE 03 — VALUE CREATION

Value Proposition Canvas & User Needs

Customer Profile
Customer Jobs: Core functional, social, and emotional tasks the client is executing within their operations.
Pains: Technical obstacles, system failure risks, structural downtime, regulatory fines, and unexpected costs.
Gains: Direct positive outcomes, accelerated delivery, certified safety, operational reliability, and asset durability.

Products & Services: Physical structural assemblies, smart IoT instrumentation, cloud software engines, and consulting.
Pain Relievers: Real-time anomaly detection, automated compliance reporting, and preventive maintenance mitigation.
Gain Creators: Substantial lifecycle cost reduction ($LCC$), optimized structural resilience, and extended service life.
MODULE 04 — NORMATIVE GOVERNANCE

Standards, Technical Codes & SLAs

Quality Management

Systematic auditing, process control, and continuous improvement across the value chain.

  • ISO 9001: QA/QC quality systems and standardized procedure control.
  • ISO 14001: Environmental management and emissions reduction.
  • ISO/IEC 15288: Complete system lifecycle processes.

Engineering Codes

Mandatory technical constraints ensuring safety, performance, and legal compliance.

  • ABNT / Eurocodes / AISC: Standardized limit state design constraints.
  • Ensures structural safety, serviceability, and long-term durability.
  • Mitigates catastrophic structural failure and legal liability.

Service Level Agreements

Legally enforceable performance indicators governing servitized operations.

  • Uptime & Availability: Guaranteed operational thresholds (e.g., 99.9% uptime).
  • Response Time (MTTR): Maximum allowable Mean Time to Repair.
  • Performance Auditing: Transparent real-time telemetry metrics.

MODULE 07 — RELIABILITY ENGINEERING

Risk Assessment, FMEA & Maintenance

Failure Modes and Effects Analysis (FMEA)

Quantitative methodology to prioritize engineering failure modes via the Risk Priority Number:

$$\text{RPN} = \text{Severity (S)} \times \text{Occurrence (O)} \times \text{Detection (D)}$$
Severity ($S \in [1, 10]$): Consequence and critical impact of the failure on structural integrity and user safety.
Occurrence ($O \in [1, 10]$): Statistical frequency and root cause probability of the failure mode happening.
Detection ($D \in [1, 10]$): Likelihood that current controls and sensor instrumentation will detect the flaw before operational delivery.
Critical Redesign Threshold: High RPN items ($\ge 200$) mandate immediate procedural redesign or hardware redundancies.

MODULE 08 — SERVITIZATION STRATEGY

Business Models & Stakeholder Co-Creation

Servitization & Value Capture

Transforming one-off transactional sales into long-term recurring contracts and performance assurance.

  • Subscription Models: Continuous software analytics and embedded edge telemetry.
  • Product-as-a-Service (PaaS): Eliminates client CAPEX; provider retains asset lifecycle risk.
  • Pay-per-use & Outcomes: Revenue tied directly to delivered operational capacity and uptime.

Stakeholder Co-Creation

Actively engaging operators, regulators, and end-users throughout the iterative engineering design cycle.

Shared Understanding: Uncovering latent constraints, field operational bottlenecks, and regulatory limits early.
Higher Market Adoption: Eliminating usability friction, stakeholder resistance, and training barriers.
Sustained Partnership: Generating long-term institutional trust, continuous telemetry feedback, and data-driven upgrades.
