MODULE 01 — FOUNDATIONS

Definition, Scope & Deliverables

Tangible Products

Physical outcomes of engineering design and manufacturing (e.g., infrastructure, machines, materials, smart devices).

  • Output-oriented deliverables
  • Physical lifecycle: production to disposal
  • Directly measurable and storable

Intangible Services

Activity-based offerings involving engineering expertise, support, monitoring, and specialized consulting.

  • Process-oriented interaction
  • Value delivered continuously over time
  • Focus on availability and uptime
CONVERGENCE PARADIGM

Product-Service Systems (PSS)

Integrated solutions combining physical hardware with real-time value-added services.

INTERACTIVE TAXONOMY (BRANCH Y=1)

Product-Service Systems (PSS) Breakdown

01. Product-Oriented

Ownership with Support

Traditional sale of physical product with maintenance contracts, spare parts, and warranty agreements.

e.g., Heavy Machinery + Maintenance SLA
02. Use-Oriented

Access over Ownership

Product ownership remains with provider; user purchases availability via leasing, renting, or sharing.

e.g., Structural Sensor Network Leasing
03. Result-Oriented

Performance Contracts

Client pays strictly for quantified outcomes or functional utility without asset ownership.

e.g., Pay-per-m³ Concrete Pumped / Thermal Comfort Level
Reference: Tukker, A. (2004). Eight types of product-service system: Eight ways to sustainability?
MODULE 02A — ECONOMIC METHODOLOGY

Life Cycle Costing (LCC)

Discounted Cash Flow Formulation

$$\text{LCC} = C_{\text{cap}} + \sum_{t=1}^{N} \frac{C_{\text{op}}(t) + C_{\text{maint}}(t)}{(1+r)^t} + \frac{C_{\text{disp}}}{(1+r)^N}$$
$C_{\text{cap}}$ (CAPEX): Initial capital cost of design, engineering, materials, acquisition, and commissioning ($t=0$).
$C_{\text{op}}(t)$ (OPEX): Annual operational expenses including energy, staffing, and consumables in year $t$.
$C_{\text{maint}}(t)$ (Maintenance): Preventive maintenance, condition monitoring, component replacements, and unscheduled repairs in year $t$.
$C_{\text{disp}}$ (Disposal/End-of-Life): Decommissioning, demolition, recycling logistics, minus net salvage value.
$r$ & $N$: Real discount/hurdle rate ($r$) and overall analytical service life horizon in years ($N$).

MODULE 02B — ENVIRONMENTAL METHODOLOGY

Life Cycle Assessment (LCA - ISO 14040/44 & EN 15978)

Environmental Impact Formulation (Global Warming Potential)

$$\text{GWP}_{\text{total}} = \text{GWP}_{\text{A1-A3}} + \text{GWP}_{\text{A4-A5}} + \text{GWP}_{\text{B1-B7}} + \text{GWP}_{\text{C1-C4}} - \text{GWP}_{\text{Module D}}$$
A1–A3 Product Stage (Cradle-to-Gate): A1 (Raw material extraction), A2 (Upstream transport), A3 (Manufacturing & refining of construction products).
A4–A5 Construction Stage: A4 (Transport of materials/systems to site), A5 (On-site installation, erection, and assembly energy).
B1–B7 Use Stage: B1 (Use), B2 (Maintenance), B3 (Repair), B4 (Replacement), B5 (Refurbishment), B6 (Operational energy use), B7 (Operational water use).
C1–C4 End-of-Life Stage: C1 (Deconstruction/demolition), C2 (Transport to disposal), C3 (Waste processing for reuse), C4 (Final disposal/landfill).
Module D (Beyond Boundary): Net environmental benefits and circular loads from reuse, recovery, and recycling potential.

MODULE 03 — VALUE CREATION

Value Proposition Canvas & User Needs

Customer Profile
Customer Jobs: Core functional, social, and emotional tasks the client is trying to execute.
Pains: Risks, technical obstacles, failures, regulatory penalties, and high downtime costs.
Gains: Concrete positive outcomes, speed, durability, safety, and certified efficiency.
Value Map (Engineering Solution)
Products & Services: Tangible structural systems, instrumentation, software, and expert consulting.
Pain Relievers: Fault mitigation, real-time alerting, automated reporting, compliance assurance.
Gain Creators: Lifecycle cost reduction, predictive structural health, optimized asset life.
MODULE 04 — NORMATIVE GOVERNANCE

Standards, Technical Codes & SLAs

Quality Management

  • ISO 9001: Process control, QA/QC, and continuous improvement.
  • ISO 14001: Environmental management and emissions control.
  • ISO/IEC 15288: System lifecycle processes (concept to retirement).

Engineering Codes

  • ABNT / Eurocodes / AISC / ASME: Mandatory technical constraints.
  • Ensures structural safety, interoperability, and durability under limit states.
  • Mitigates legal liability and technical failures.

Service Level Agreements

  • Uptime & Availability: Quantitative commitment (e.g., 99.9% system availability).
  • Response Time (MTTR): Maximum allowable time to repair faults.
  • Performance Metrics: Continuous auditing and client transparency.

MODULE 07 — RELIABILITY ENGINEERING

Risk Assessment, FMEA & Maintenance

Failure Modes and Effects Analysis (FMEA)

Quantitative methodology to prioritize engineering failure modes via the Risk Priority Number:

$$\text{RPN} = \text{Severity (S)} \times \text{Occurrence (O)} \times \text{Detection (D)}$$

Where $S, O, D \in [1, 10]$. High RPN items mandate immediate structural or procedural redesign.

MODULE 08 — SERVITIZATION STRATEGY

Business Models & Stakeholder Co-Creation

Servitization & Value Capture

Transforming one-off transactional sales into long-term recurring service contracts.

  • Subscription Models: Continuous software & sensor analytics.
  • Product-as-a-Service (PaaS): Zero initial client CAPEX.
  • Pay-per-use: Revenue tied directly to system output.

Stakeholder Co-Creation

Actively engaging operators, regulators, and end-users throughout the iterative design cycle.

Shared Understanding: Uncovering latent constraints early.
Higher Adoption: Eliminating usability friction and resistance.
Long-term Trust: Sustained client engagement and continuous feedback.
